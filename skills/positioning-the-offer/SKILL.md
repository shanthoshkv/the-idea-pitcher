# Positioning the offer

Write `pitches/<slug>/positioning.md`. Do not draft the pitch here. Do not invent a competitor or a market size.

Read `brief.md`, `competition.md`, `skills/pitching-an-idea/references/claim-rules.md`, and `skills/pitching-an-idea/references/artifact-layout.md`. If `competition.md` is missing, stop. The alternatives live there.

Method: April Dunford, "A Quickstart Guide to Positioning" (15 March 2021), https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning. The article is not in this repo. Do not copy her numbers into this file.

Fill the sections in order. The sentence is last. A fill-in-the-blank positioning statement is not a way to find the position.

```markdown
# Positioning

## 1. Alternatives the customer actually uses
Copied from competition.md, including the status quo.
Left out, because the buyer does not meet them in the deal:

## 2. What we can do that those alternatives do not

## 3. So what
For each line in section 2, the value in the job on the brief. A feature with no so-what is cut.

## 4. Who cares a lot
Narrower than the brief when the value only matters to some of them.

## 5. Category
An existing category, narrowed so section 3 is obvious.
New category only if the user insisted. Tag that line ASSUMPTION, and name what the buyer has to learn before the word means anything.

## Sentence
<category> for <who>, instead of <alternatives>, because <so what>.
```

An alternative is what the customer would do if this offer did not exist. That includes the spreadsheet, the intern, a hire, and doing nothing. A company that could compete on paper, and that the buyer never meets, stays in `competition.md` and stays out of section 1. Say that you left it out.

Section 2 is only a difference against section 1. A capability the status quo already has is not a difference.

Section 5 uses a category the buyer already understands, then narrows it. Shape: "<known category> for <the customer who cares>." A new word is the expensive path. Use it only when the user insisted, and mark it ASSUMPTION.

## When they push

| Excuse | What you do instead |
| --- | --- |
| Write the positioning statement first | Sections 1 to 5, in that order. The sentence is last. |
| We have no competitors | Copy the status quo from competition.md. |
| List every company in the space | Section 1 is who the buyer would turn to. A company they never meet is left out, with a line saying so. |
| Invent a category so we look unique | Narrow a category they already know. |
| Put a market-share number in the sentence | No number that is not already a FACT in the research files. |
