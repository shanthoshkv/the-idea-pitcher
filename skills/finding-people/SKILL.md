# Finding people

Write `pitches/<slug>/people.md`. Read the brief, `skills/pitching-an-idea/references/genres.md`, `skills/pitching-an-idea/references/claim-rules.md`, and `skills/pitching-an-idea/references/artifact-layout.md`.

Search the live web for rooms where this brief's customer already talks about this job. Use the genre row as a search hint. Do not paste a standing list of communities. A room without a URL you opened is UNKNOWN.

If search is unavailable, stop. Do not name rooms from memory.

```markdown
# People

- Brief: pitches/<slug>/brief.md
- Searched: YYYY-MM-DD
- Who-where: <role or person> who <did the job> in the last month, and they gather at <rooms or UNKNOWN>.

## 1. People the founder already knows
Names only if the founder gave them. Otherwise: ask for two names, and write "not provided."

## 2. Introductions
Who could introduce the founder to the buyer. Founder-provided, or UNKNOWN. Friends are a bridge. They are a weak source of honesty.

## 3. Public complaints
Paraphrase, URL, and the words the buyer used. Queries and "none found" when a search is empty.

## 4. Rooms
### <room name>
- URL:
- Why this customer is here:
- Rules that matter: quote or paraphrase from the room's own rules page, with URL. If you found no rules page, UNKNOWN.

## 5. Named strangers
Up to 5 public profiles that match the who-where sentence. Name, role, URL. No email address. No phone number. If you cannot see a public page, do not list the person.

## 6. One partner
A person or organization that already has this buyer's trust: an accountant, a tool, an association, a consultant. URL or UNKNOWN.

## Reach
Copy Reach from the brief. If it is starting-from-zero, say the first move is section 1 or 4, not a cold list.
```

Do not contact anyone. Do not collect addresses.
