# Claim rules

Tag every non-obvious claim. A claim with no tag is unfinished.

| Tag | Use when | Must include |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FACT | A page you opened says this | URL, the date you read it, and the publisher |
| INFERENCE | A conclusion from tags already in the file | Which FACT or ASSUMPTION lines it uses |
| ASSUMPTION | The founder said it, or you are guessing | Who owns it: founder or model |
| UNKNOWN | Search missed it, or the founder does not know | The query you ran, or "not provided" |

## Numbers

Market size, price, funding, headcount, growth, and share need a FACT tag. No source means UNKNOWN. Do not estimate a figure and call it research.

Bottom-up math is allowed only as INFERENCE. Every input is a FACT or a founder ASSUMPTION. Show the multiplication. Do not promote the result to a TAM headline.

A top-down figure is a FACT only with publisher, year, and URL. It is the report's market, not this company's market.

SOM is a plan for who you reach first. It is not a percentage of a TAM.

## Competitors

Names come from pages you opened. If a query returns nothing, write the query and "none found." Do not treat that as an empty market.

Always name the status quo: the spreadsheet, the manual chore, the agency, an employee, or doing nothing.

A weakness needs a review, a doc, a pricing page, or the founder's own experience. Tag founder experience as ASSUMPTION. Do not invent a weakness to flatter the idea.

## Interviews

A conversation is a FACT only when the founder pasted the notes and said it happened. Name the person as they named them, and the date they gave. You did not conduct the interview.

If they did not paste notes, do not write a conversation file. You may still list questions for them to ask.

## Thin evidence

When the load-bearing claims are ASSUMPTION or UNKNOWN, the verdict is `do-not-pitch-yet`. Name the single cheapest test that would change it.

## Critic

The critic does not rewrite the pitch and does not encourage. "What already holds up" is allowed only when each point cites a FACT.
